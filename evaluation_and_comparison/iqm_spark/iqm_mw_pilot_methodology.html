Goal of the pilot

The Meyer-Wallach workflow estimates the average entanglement produced by each ansatz/depth configuration under random parameter draws. The pilot is not just a runtime convenience. It defines a quantitative precision rule for choosing:

  • shots: precision of each measured Pauli expectation,
  • n_samples: precision of the random-parameter average,
  • iterations: run-to-run stability under real QPU drift.

The runner is: 

python scripts/run_iqm_mw_pilot.py --pilot-id mw_pilot_paper

What the MW score estimates

For an \(n\)-qubit pure state, the Meyer-Wallach score is

$$ Q(|\psi\rangle) = \frac{2}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} \left(1-\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_i^2)\right), $$

where \(\rho_i\) is the one-qubit reduced density matrix of qubit \(i\). The key simplification is that MW does not require full state tomography. It only requires the one-qubit reduced density matrices.

Each one-qubit marginal is reconstructed from its Bloch vector:

$$ \rho_i = \frac{1}{2} \left(I+x_iX+y_iY+z_iZ\right), $$ $$ x_i=\langle X_i\rangle,\qquad y_i=\langle Y_i\rangle,\qquad z_i=\langle Z_i\rangle. $$

The purity is

$$ \operatorname{Tr}(\rho_i^2) = \frac{1}{2} \left(1+x_i^2+y_i^2+z_i^2\right), $$

so the hardware-estimated MW score can be written as

$$ Q = 1-\frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} \left(x_i^2+y_i^2+z_i^2\right). $$
Each random parameter sample therefore needs three circuits: one for the \(Z\)-basis measurement, one for the \(X\)-basis measurement, and one for the \(Y\)-basis measurement.

What mw_std, mw_sem, and 95% half-width mean

For a fixed ansatz/depth configuration, the runner draws \(N\) random parameter vectors and obtains one MW score per draw:

$$ Q_1,\ Q_2,\ \dots,\ Q_N. $$

The reported mean is

$$ \bar Q = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{j=1}^{N}Q_j. $$

mw_std

The sample standard deviation of the individual MW scores:

$$ s_Q = \sqrt{ \frac{1}{N-1} \sum_{j=1}^{N} (Q_j-\bar Q)^2 }. $$

It measures how much entanglement varies across random parameter vectors. It is not the uncertainty of the final mean.

mw_sem

The standard error of the mean:

$$ \operatorname{SEM}(\bar Q) = \frac{s_Q}{\sqrt{N}}. $$

This measures how precisely the experiment estimates the mean MW for that ansatz/depth configuration.

95% half-width

The approximate 95% confidence half-width for the mean:

$$ h_{95} \approx 1.96\operatorname{SEM}(\bar Q). $$

The reported mean is then summarized as \(\bar Q \pm h_{95}\), approximately.

A large mw_std is not necessarily bad. It can mean that the ansatz produces a broad range of entanglement values over random parameters. A large mw_sem, however, means that the mean MW is not yet estimated precisely.

Shot noise and the shot-count rule

For one qubit \(i\) and Pauli basis \(P\in\{X,Y,Z\}\), each shot is a binary outcome \(Y_s\in\{-1,+1\}\). The Pauli expectation estimator is

$$ \hat p_{iP} = \frac{1}{S} \sum_{s=1}^{S}Y_s. $$

Since \(Y_s^2=1\), if \(p_{iP}=\mathbb E[Y_s]\), then

$$ \operatorname{Var}(\hat p_{iP}) = \frac{1-p_{iP}^2}{S} \leq \frac{1}{S}. $$

The MW score depends on squared Pauli expectations,

$$ Q = 1-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i,P}p_{iP}^2. $$

Applying the delta method,

$$ \operatorname{Var}(\hat Q) \approx \sum_{i,P} \left( \frac{2p_{iP}}{n} \right)^2 \frac{1-p_{iP}^2}{S}. $$

Using the Bloch-vector constraint \(x_i^2+y_i^2+z_i^2\leq1\), we get the conservative single-sample shot-noise scale

$$ \operatorname{SD}_{\mathrm{shot}}(\hat Q) \lesssim \frac{2}{\sqrt{nS}}. $$

For \(n=5\) and \(S=1024\), as in the current final run,

$$ \frac{2}{\sqrt{5\cdot1024}} \approx 0.028. $$

Averaging over \(N=60\) random parameter samples reduces the mean-level contribution to

$$ \frac{0.028}{\sqrt{60}} \approx 0.0036. $$
The pilot shot rule checks whether increasing shots changes the observed mean MW by less than a tolerance, by default \(0.02\) MW units.

Sample-size rule

Once shots are fixed, the dominant uncertainty is usually Monte Carlo variation over random parameter draws. The pilot uses the half-width rule

$$ h_N = 1.96\frac{s_Q}{\sqrt{N}} \leq h_{\mathrm{target}}. $$

The default target is

$$ h_{\mathrm{target}}=0.03. $$

Rearranging gives the required number of samples:

$$ N_{\mathrm{req}} = \left\lceil \left( \frac{1.96\,s_Q}{h_{\mathrm{target}}} \right)^2 \right\rceil. $$

This is why simply increasing samples can make a mean more precise, but the cost grows quadratically with the desired reduction in half-width.

Comparing two ansatze

To compare ODRA and simulator at the same depth, define the difference

$$ d = \bar Q_{\mathrm{ODRA}} - \bar Q_{\mathrm{Simulator}}. $$

Assuming independent random-parameter samples, the standard error of the difference is

$$ \operatorname{SE}(d) = \sqrt{ \operatorname{SEM}_{\mathrm{ODRA}}^2 + \operatorname{SEM}_{\mathrm{Simulator}}^2 }. $$

The approximate 95% half-width for the difference is

$$ h_{95}(d) \approx 1.96\,\operatorname{SE}(d). $$

If \(|d| \ll h_{95}(d)\), then the two means are not distinguishable at this precision. If \(|d|\) is larger than the half-width, the observed difference is resolvable statistically, but should still be checked against QPU drift if the effect is small.

Hardware iterations and drift

The statistics above use random-parameter uncertainty. They do not include time-dependent QPU drift. To estimate drift, repeat the frozen final protocol with the same seed and choose the smallest number of iterations \(K\) whose worst-case 95% half-width meets a drift target.

For repeated hardware sweeps \(\bar Q_1,\bar Q_2,\dots,\bar Q_K\) at fixed shots and \(N\), define the iteration standard deviation \(s_{\mathrm{iter}}\) and Student-\(t\) 95% half-width

$$ h_{\mathrm{iter}} = t_{0.975,K-1}\, \frac{s_{\mathrm{iter}}}{\sqrt{K}}. $$

Choose the smallest \(K \ge K_{\min}\) such that

$$ \max_{\text{ansatz, depth}} h_{\mathrm{iter}} \le h_{\mathrm{target}}. $$

Defaults are --min-iterations 3, --max-iterations 5, and --target-iteration-half-width 0.01. Use 0.02 for qualitative depth trends.

After shots and samples are already frozen, run only the drift pilot: 

python scripts/run_iqm_mw_pilot.py \
  --drift-only \
  --shots 1024 \
  --samples 60 \
  --target-iteration-half-width 0.01 \
  --min-iterations 3 \
  --max-iterations 5 \
  --pilot-id mw_drift_pilot_final

Because the seed is fixed, the random parameter vectors are reused. Iteration-to-iteration changes then mainly reflect calibration drift, queue-time effects, residual shot noise, and other hardware variability.

Report both:

  • \(\operatorname{SEM}\) or 95% half-width across random samples, and
  • \(h_{\mathrm{iter}}\) across repeated frozen hardware sweeps.
