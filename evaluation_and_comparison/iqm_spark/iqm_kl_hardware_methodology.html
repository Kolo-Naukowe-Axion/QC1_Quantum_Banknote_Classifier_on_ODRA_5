KL Expressibility on IQM Spark

Fixed-budget hardware protocol with rigorous uncertainty: percentile bootstrap on fidelity pairs, optional repeat run for drift, and simulator reference on the same seeds. Depths 2 and 4 only; depth 6 excluded from QPU KL.

Koło Naukowe Axion · ODRA 5 (IQM Spark) · Companion to iqm_kl_pilot_methodology.md (legacy half-width pilot) and iqm_mw_pilot_methodology.html (Meyer–Wallach).

Recommended package (rigorous + literature-aligned). One QPU sweep at $N = 60$ pairs per (ansatz, depth) with $S = 2048$ tomography shots (same wall-time budget as $N = 30$ at 4096 shots) and 90% percentile bootstrap CI on resampled fidelities; one repeat execution ($K=2$) for day-to-day stability; simulator KL on the same seeds for expressibility reference. Total KL QPU budget: $\approx 8\text{–}16$ h (depths $\{2,4\}$ only).

“Lower KL divergence indicates better expressibility, meaning a better ability to explore a wider range of states in the Hilbert space.”

[3] Saib et al. (2023), on Sim et al.’s Expr score (arXiv:2309.06975).

Scope and design principles

This document defines the production methodology for measuring KL-style expressibility on IQM Spark when both a point estimate and defensible uncertainty are required, without running the multi-stage half-width calibration pilot (~40–100+ h QPU).

In scope

  • Ansätze: ansatz_odra, ansatz_simulator
  • Depths: 2 and 4 only
  • $n = 5$ qubits; full $3^n$ Pauli tomography per state
  • Hardware–hardware overlaps $F = \mathrm{Tr}(\rho_a \rho_b)$

Out of scope

  • Depth 6 on QPU (classifier collapse; not used for KL budget)
  • Shot-grid pilot (512…8192 sweeps)
  • Iteration pilot tuned to $h_{\mathrm{iter}} \le 0.02$
  • Choosing $N$ until bootstrap half-width $\le 0.03$

Three uncertainty layers are reported separately (do not merge into one “95% band”):

  1. Finite pair count — percentile bootstrap on $\{F_j\}$ (offline, no extra QPU).
  2. Input spread — mean and std of fidelities $\{F_j\}$ (descriptive, not a CI on KL).
  3. Day-to-day drift — difference between two identical protocol executions ($K=2$).
Citation — separate uncertainty layers (bootstrap vs repeat-run dispersion)

“We use non-parametric bootstrapping with 10,000 bootstrap samples to calculate the variance of the zero noise limit result.”

[1] A. Ash Saki et al., “Hypothesis Testing for Error Mitigation,” arXiv:2301.02690 (2023).

“We repeat ‘experiments’: the entire process of state preparation and generating $N_{\mathrm{shots}}$ samples several times ($N_{\mathrm{experiments}}$). For each experiment, we compute the values of our quality of solution metrics, and then report the mean and standard deviation as above.”

[2] S. Marsh et al., “Cross-Platform Benchmarking of Near-Term Quantum Optimisation Algorithms,” arXiv:2504.06885 (2025).
Citation — fixed budget instead of adaptive half-width pilot

“Sim et al. employed 5000 fidelity samples for four-qubit circuits to measure expressibility. This reliance on a large number of samples poses challenges due to the time and computational resources required.”

[3] A. Saib et al., “Predicting Expressibility of Parameterized Quantum Circuits using Graph Neural Network,” arXiv:2309.06975 (2023).

Quantity being estimated

For fixed ansatz and circuit depth, draw independent random parameter pairs $(\theta_a^{(j)}, \theta_b^{(j)})$ and prepare two states on hardware. After tomography, obtain fidelities $F_j \in [0,1]$. Following Sim et al., compare the empirical histogram of $\{F_j\}$ to the Haar-random analytic fidelity density on $[0,1]$:

$$ p_{\mathrm{Haar}}(F) = (d-1)(1-F)^{d-2}, \qquad d = 2^n. $$

Discretize $[0,1]$ into $B$ equal bins, form normalized histogram masses $\hat{p}_k$ and reference masses $q_k$, stabilize empty bins with $\varepsilon = 10^{-12}$, and define the plug-in expressibility score:

$$ \widehat{D}_{\mathrm{KL}} = \sum_{k=1}^{B} \tilde{p}_k \ln\frac{\tilde{p}_k}{\tilde{q}_k}. $$

Lower $\widehat{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}$ indicates closer agreement with Haar-random state-space exploration at the tested depth. Implementation: compute_kl_for_fidelities in src/qbanknote/metrics.py.

One KL per configuration. Unlike Meyer–Wallach (where each random parameter draw yields one score $ \hat{Q}_j $), a single hardware run with $N$ pairs produces one scalar $\widehat{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}$ from the full histogram. Uncertainty over random pairs is assessed by bootstrap resampling of $\{F_j\}$, not by a sample standard deviation of multiple KL values from the same run.
Citation — KL expressibility metric and Haar reference

“For the ensemble of Haar random states, the analytical form of the probability density function of fidelities is known: $P_{\mathrm{Haar}}(F)=(N-1)(1-F)^{N-2}$ … the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence … between the estimated fidelity distribution and that of the Haar-distributed ensemble can be computed to quantify expressibility (‘Expr’): $\mathrm{Expr}=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\hat{P}_{\mathrm{PQC}}(F;\boldsymbol{\theta})\,\|\,P_{\mathrm{Haar}}(F))$.”

[4] S. Sim, P. D. Johnson, A. Aspuru-Guzik, “Expressibility and entangling capability of parameterized quantum circuits,” Adv. Quantum Technol. 2, 1900070 (2019).

“In practice, we can estimate the fidelity distributions … by independently sampling pairs of states (i.e. sampling pairs of parameter vectors and obtaining parameterized states) and treating the corresponding fidelities as random variables.”

[4] Sim et al. (2019), arXiv:1905.10876.

“Due to a finite sample size, the probability distribution is estimated with a histogram. Therefore, we must choose a discretization of the two probability distributions in order to numerically estimate the KL divergence. By this scoring method, a PQC with a resulting fidelity distribution that corresponds to a lower KL divergence with respect to that of Haar, is a more expressible circuit.”

[4] Sim et al. (2019), arXiv:1905.10876.

Fidelity acquisition on IQM

Each fidelity pair requires full tensor-product Pauli-basis state tomography for both states:

$$ N_{\mathrm{circuits\ per\ pair}} = 2 \times 3^n. $$

For $n=5$: $2 \times 243 = 486$ circuits per pair. Reconstruct $\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{lin}}$, project to $\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{phys}}$, then

$$ F_j = \mathrm{Tr}(\hat{\rho}_{a,\mathrm{phys}} \hat{\rho}_{b,\mathrm{phys}}). $$

Primary input for KL analysis: column fidelity_physical in iqm_kl_fidelities.csv. Tomography details match full_odra_fidelity.ipynb and Dania.pdf Part V–VII.

All knobs are fixed before the QPU campaign (no adaptive stopping). Bin count $B$ is chosen offline from Haar-random draws (Stage 1 in iqm_kl_pilot_methodology.md); shot count may be aligned with the completed Meyer–Wallach pilot.

Primary uncertainty: percentile bootstrap

Question answered: “Given only $N$ hardware fidelity pairs, how much could $\widehat{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}$ move if we resampled the same underlying distribution?”

Procedure

  1. Collect $\{F_1,\ldots,F_N\}$ from one completed sweep.
  2. Compute point estimate $\widehat{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}$ from all $N$ values.
  3. For $b = 1,\ldots,B_{\mathrm{boot}}$ (recommended $B_{\mathrm{boot}} = 5000\text{–}10000$):
    • Draw $N$ indices with replacement from $\{1,\ldots,N\}$.
    • Form resampled set $\{F_j^*\}$ and recompute $D_{\mathrm{KL}}^{*(b)}$.
  4. Report percentile interval (not $\widehat{D} \pm 1.96\,\sigma_{\mathrm{boot}}$):
    • 90% CI: $[Q_{5\%}, Q_{95\%}]$ of $\{D_{\mathrm{KL}}^{*(b)}\}$
    • 95% CI: $[Q_{2.5\%}, Q_{97.5\%}]$
Why not $1.96 \times \mathrm{std}$ (legacy pilot)? The bootstrap distribution of $D_{\mathrm{KL}}$ is often skewed for small $N$: resampling sparse histograms shifts KL upward. Symmetric normal intervals centered on $\widehat{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}$ can understate the upper tail. Percentile bootstrap matches practice in randomized benchmarking and error-mitigation papers, which report asymmetric intervals from resampled data.
Citation — percentile (quantile) bootstrap intervals

“The BCa procedure is a method of setting approximate confidence intervals for $\theta$ from the percentiles of the bootstrap histogram … In this case the 0.90 BCa interval is the interval between the 5th and 95th percentiles of the bootstrap replications.”

[8] T. J. DiCiccio and B. Efron, “Bootstrap confidence intervals,” Statist. Sci. 11, 189–228 (1996); doi:10.1214/ss/1032280214.

“To obtain confidence intervals on the step error … we perform bias-corrected parametric bootstrapping with 10,000 bootstrap samples … The bootstrap histograms, point estimates, and 68% bootstrapped confidence intervals are shown in Fig. 4.”

[9] C. Ryan-Anderson et al., “Optimized experiment design and analysis for fully randomized benchmarking,” arXiv:2312.15836 (2023).

“first we resample the list of sequences with replacement, and then for each sequence we binomially resample the success and failure counts.”

[9] Ryan-Anderson et al. (2023) on resampling-with-replacement bootstrap replications (arXiv:2312.15836).

“We use non-parametric bootstrapping with 10,000 bootstrap samples to calculate the variance of the zero noise limit result.”

[1] Ash Saki et al. (2023) on bootstrap reanalysis of hardware-derived expectation values (arXiv:2301.02690).

Pseudocode

fidelities = load("iqm_kl_fidelities.csv")  # fidelity_physical, N rows
kl_hat = compute_kl(fidelities, d=32, B_bins=400, eps=1e-12)

kl_boot = []
for b in 1..B_boot:
    draw = resample_with_replacement(fidelities, size=N)
    kl_boot.append(compute_kl(draw, d=32, B_bins=400, eps=1e-12))

ci_90 = [quantile(kl_boot, 0.05), quantile(kl_boot, 0.95)]
ci_95 = [quantile(kl_boot, 0.025), quantile(kl_boot, 0.975)]

Implemented in bootstrap_kl_interval / compute_kl_bootstrap_uncertainty and scripts/analyze_iqm_kl_hardware.py. Legacy pilot code uses bootstrap_kl_std and kl_confidence_half_width = 1.96 * std.

Secondary uncertainty: repeat run ($K=2$)

Question answered: “How much does $\widehat{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}$ change between independent executions of the same protocol (hardware drift, queue, calibration)?”

Run the identical frozen protocol twice (same seed, $N$, $S$, $B$, ansätze, depths). Store under separate run directories, e.g.: 

iqm_kl_outputs/kl_hardware_run1/
iqm_kl_outputs/kl_hardware_run2/

Report per (ansatz, depth):

  • $\widehat{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}^{(1)}$, $\widehat{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}^{(2)}$
  • $\Delta_{\mathrm{drift}} = \bigl|\widehat{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}^{(1)} - \widehat{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}^{(2)}\bigr|$
  • Optional: mean $\bar{D} = \frac{1}{2}(D^{(1)}+D^{(2)})$ and sample std over $K=2$ (interpret cautiously at small $K$)
Do not use Student-$t$ iteration half-width with $K=2$ as an acceptance rule ($t_{0.975,1} \approx 12.7$). Two runs give a drift range, not a tight 95% metrology interval. This matches IQM/VQE papers that report $E \pm \sigma$ over a small number of executions without calibrating $K$ to a half-width target.
Citation — repeat identical protocol ($K=2$) and run-to-run dispersion

“We repeat ‘experiments’: the entire process of state preparation and generating $N_{\mathrm{shots}}$ samples several times ($N_{\mathrm{experiments}}$). For each experiment, we compute the values of our quality of solution metrics, and then report the mean and standard deviation as above to capture this variation in state preparation.”

[2] Marsh et al. (2025), arXiv:2504.06885.

“Run-to-run standard deviations are reported to characterize landscape ruggedness.”

[10] VQE ansatz benchmarking under noise (reports multiple independent hardware/sim runs); arXiv:2606.04955 (2026).

“The circuits that have been optimised identically are then placed on the IQM Garnet quantum processor, using a resource-efficient batched protocol.”

[11] IQM hardware repeat-run context (Ising model VQE on IQM Garnet); arXiv:2601.17515 (2026).

QPU budget

With depths $\{2,4\}$ only and ~4 min per pair: $ \text{hours} \approx N_{\mathrm{pairs,sweep}} / 15 $.

What this protocol replaces

The legacy run_iqm_kl_pilot.py pipeline chooses $N$, $K$, and shots by requiring bootstrap / Student-$t$ 95% half-widths below fixed targets ($h \le 0.03$, $h_{\mathrm{iter}} \le 0.02$). That is metrology-style experimental design, not the expressibility literature standard, and typically requires ~40–100+ h QPU for tomography-based KL.

“Parametric or non-parametric bias-corrected bootstrapping can be used to obtain confidence intervals for one or more parameters … at moderate confidence levels, the intervals obtained are useful for interpretation but should be treated as approximate.”

Commands and artifacts

1. Offline bin choice (no QPU)

python scripts/run_iqm_kl_pilot.py \
  --skip-shots --skip-iterations \
  --max-samples 30 --pilot-id kl_bins_only

2. Primary hardware sweep (single run-id, K=2 iterations)

export IQM_TOKEN="..."
bash scripts/run_iqm_kl_hardware_study.sh

Or invoke the expressibility runner directly (writes iteration_1/ and iteration_2/ under one run-id):

python scripts/run_iqm_kl_expressibility.py \
  --run-id kl_hardware \
  --depth 2 4 \
  --samples 60 \
  --shots 2048 \
  --n-bins 400 \
  --seed 42 \
  --iterations 2 \
  --skip-iteration-precision

3. Repeat sweep (run 2, day-to-day drift)

Either use --iterations 2 above, or run a second day with a separate run-id and pass --compare-run-dir to analysis (see below).

SKIP_QPU=1 SKIP_ANALYSIS=0 \
  RUN_ID=kl_hardware_day2 ITERATIONS=1 \
  bash scripts/run_iqm_kl_hardware_study.sh
